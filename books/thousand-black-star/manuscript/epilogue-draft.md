# EPILOGUE (draft)

## Chapter 60 — The Sixty-First Letter (closing pages)

*Cornwall. A year to the night. Edmund Ashby, eighty-one, sits in Lantern House with Nadeesha Fernando beside him, and a letter in her hands that has been waiting in a lamp for a year.*

> **ENTRY 1000 — Colombo / Cornwall.**
>
> *Dear Edmund,*
>
> *You will say I am late. I have been late once before, in nineteen seventy-seven, on a railway platform, when I did not say what I should have said while the train was still in the station. So let me say it first, before the clocks, and before the wind, and before you have the chance to be honourable at me.*
>
> *I was not angry. I was not ever angry. I was only afraid, as you were afraid, and for the same reason, which is that we were both of us certain we would love the other too much, and neither of us could bear to be the one who*

*Here the page is torn.*

---

## The Chorus Speaks the Epilogue

> The page is torn. We do not know the rest;
> It is the Keeper's, and the Keeper's gone.
> And yet you are not left with least or best
> But all the stars that wait before the dawn.
> Some loves were crowned, and some were laid in earth,
> And some broke off mid-word and are not mended;
> And some, half-lit, in lands of different birth,
> Sit patiently, until the night is ended.
> For out in Phuket, on a beach of white,
> Where neither flag nor creed nor country stands,
> A girl whose hands are stained with indigo light
> Meets one who walks the world with open hands.
> What passed between them, we shall not discover:
> The ledger keeps a place, and keeps it for another.

---

*Margin note, in pencil, beside Entries 0713 and 0714, in the Keeper's hand, added in the final week of her life and read by Nadeesha one year later:*

> **"Met. See the next ledger."**

*THE END*

*(of this book. The Register of Black Stars is closed at one thousand — but the dark is never full.)*

# THOUSAND BLACK STAR

*A Novel of Sixty Loves Beneath One Dark Sky*

**Al Fayaz**

---

*For everyone who has kept a sentence behind their teeth.*

---

## PROLOGUE
### *Spoken by the Chorus*

In heaven's field a thousand stars lie dark,
Not dead, but dimmed by words they dared not speak;
Yet each doth bend the light about its arc
And draws the wandering heart to what they seek.
Come, then, kind friends, and lend a patient ear:
We tell of sixty loves beneath one sky,
Of some made glad, and some that drown in fear,
Of some that break mid-word and leave no why.
A boy from Dhaka, banks of river-rain,
A girl whose islands rise from Java's sea,
Shall write across the dark their long refrain,
And ask the night what yet may come to be.
Not all shall end as hearts would have them end—
Yet read: the dark hath light enough to lend.

## ACT II — THE HOUSES AT WAR
*Winter*
### *The Chorus*

When frost has closed the harbours of the heart,
Old quarrels wake, like wolves behind a wall;
And house on house, with envy set apart,
Will stake the young upon the pride of all.
Two ferries cross one strait with hostile prow;
Two shops divide one street with hostile eyes;
Yet love, that knows no feud, nor flag, nor vow,
Slips through the fence beneath the winter skies.
Some shall be saved by what their fathers spurned;
Some shall be lost to what their fathers swore;
And some shall wait where all their letters burned,
And hear the tide knock softly at the door.
Then mark the cold; it tests what will not fail,
And what is true outlives the winter's gale.

---

## ACT III — A MIDSUMMER CONFUSION
*Spring*
### *The Chorus*

Now falls the night when all the clocks go wrong,
When masks are worn by those who need no mask,
And lovers send by wire what once was song,
And answer questions no one dared to ask.
A boy awake beneath the Bengal moon,
A girl one hour ahead of him in time,
Shall speak by light of glass, and none too soon,
While distant bells of doubt and duty chime.
Not all that shines is seen; not all that's seen
Is true; and what is true is hard to hold:
The dark is kind, the mask is thin, between
The two a heart may choose to grow or fold.
So hush; the hour is late, the night is deep,
And love hath promised more than sleep can keep.

---

## ACT IV — WHAT TIME TAKES
*Summer*
### *The Chorus*

Time, that devours the child and feeds the old,
Walks softly through the summer's heavy hours;
He steals the voice, the hearing, and the gold,
And leaves instead a handful of dry flowers.
Yet mark how time restores what time hath torn,
Not in the shape it left, but changed and grown;
The seed laid down in winter greets the morn,
The wandering heart returns to claim its own.
But some shall wait, and wait, and not be paid;
And some shall sing their last in empty halls;
For summer's wage is kind, but long delayed,
And every light at last to dark must fall.
So bear with Time: he steals, but he is just;
And what he spares was meant to outlast dust.

---

## ACT V — THE TEMPEST CLEARS
*Autumn*
### *The Chorus*

The storm is spent; the sea lies glass and grey,
And one by one the ships come home to shore;
What burned is ash, what drowned is borne away,
And what was owed is paid, and owed no more.
Now open every letter ever kept,
Now speak the word that waited, locked and still,
For here are those who laughed, and those who wept,
And those who learned at last to bend their will.
Look up: the dark is full of unseen light;
Each hidden star hath held its steadfast place;
And one, the thousandth, burning out of sight,
Was lit for you, and keeps you in its grace.
So close the book, but leave the window wide:
The dark is not a wall; it is a guide.

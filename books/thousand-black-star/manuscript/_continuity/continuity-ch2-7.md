# Continuity notes, Chapters 2-7

## Ch 2 (Lisbon, S01)
- Tomás's letter to Inês was addressed to "SENHORA, RUA DA SAUDADE 14, LISBOA" (street does not exist); he reasoned dead letters go to the desk of the postwoman who annotates them. Posted about seven months before Ch 1; signed only with a small pencil drawing of a wheel.
- Letter's second line: "I am a man who mends things that carry others, and I would like, once, to be carried." First line: "To the lady who writes small kindnesses on the backs of other people's envelopes."
- Inês's trademark: pencils small notes on the backs of misdelivered envelopes; her yellow satchel and red bicycle (pushed, not ridden).
- Register scans show handwriting; Inês matched Tomás's square block capitals (#0753) to the letter she kept.
- Rita Rocha (#0117, Inês's sister, journalist) found Inês's unposted letter in the kitchen drawer (under the string) and mailed it to the Keeper; this is how #0053 exists. Inês did not know until Ch 2.
- Rui Costa (#0381, plumber, Inês's ex of 11 months at her age 26) falsely claims authorship; his handwriting has a curled "4" and a kicking "R".
- Tomás works at the Santo Amaro tram depot; his tram is old yellow carriage "Dona Conceição" (98 years old), tram 28.
- Nuno Pereira (#0520) tells Tomás the postwoman is 60 with a moustache.
- Pereira the lamp-seller sells brass lamps (80 euros); drives the comic thread; has no Register number.
- Ch 2 ending: Inês and Tomás meet at Largo das Portas do Sol; neither admits the letter yet; she holds the envelope to her chest and says she will "check the address". He knows only that she is "Inês, the post"; she knows only "Tomás". Ch 12 must pay off the letter handover.
- Dead letters are kept seven years then burned (invented postal rule).

## Ch 3 (Accra, S02)
- Setting: Palm Lane (invented), Adabraka area, 80 yards between Owusu Chemist & Dispensary (north end, cream building, green shutters) and Mensah Cabs (south end, yellow corrugated gate, 41 taxis and a goat).
- Feud began six years ago with parking and signs ("NO TAXIS. THIS IS A PLACE OF HEALING" / "AND THIS IS A PLACE OF GETTING THERE").
- Abena's father Kojo Darko (#0807) founded the dispensary, remortgaged it twice on a cocoa scheme, died in June six years ago; Abena (then 22) took her mother's name Owusu and put it over the door. Bank debt: 42,000 cedis, settled by an unnamed "gentleman, tall, who laughed at his own car" (Kwame).
- Kwame also secretly paid her rent for 71 months in cash on the first of every month via landlady Naa Tetteh (#0963), disguised as "a clerical error".
- Abena's repayment: a round blue Danish butter-biscuit tin, left on the bonnet of an old green Corolla called "Auntie Beatrice". Kwame has not counted or opened the money. Label inside lid: "TAKE AS DIRECTED... (Eighty yards. I counted.)"
- Quaye (senior driver, 58, no Register number) leads "the Rank" (taxi chorus); has a roof loudspeaker. Yaw Addo (#0231, 23) is a driver/waiter.
- Kwame's mother Akosua Tetteh (#0007); Nii Tetteh (#0828) his choir-director friend; Ama Tetteh (#0811, 82) saw him pay; Adwoa Tetteh (#0835) runs the kiosk next to the pharmacy and is Abena's best friend.
- Harmattan arrives early this year.
- Kwame is not a pharmacist; Quaye jokes he "dispenses lifts" (reconciles Ch 1's "two pharmacists").
- Not resolved: tin stays unopened; Ch 13 and 50 must carry the arc to marriage.

## Ch 4 (Osaka, S03)
- Tanabe-ya: eight-stool ramen counter in an alley behind the Tenma canal; broth simmered 14 hours (kombu, pork bone, dried bonito).
- Hiroshi sets one bowl at stool eight (kept warm by a small heated cushion) at 11:40 each night, pours it away at midnight; 426 nights so far (14 months).
- Mei's illness: a fever in September of the previous year; lost taste entirely, can still smell lemon; column "A Hundred Bowls" in the Naniwa Shimbun (Sunday); she has been writing from memory. Editor Mr. Kato (#0423 Sora Kato) suspects; Sakura Takahashi (#0702, newsagent) is her snide rival.
- Hiroshi knew: her Namba-shop column said the broth was "a clear autumn pond" though theirs is thick.
- Mei sat at seat eight every Thursday for two years before the fever. She confessed her loss to Hiroshi in Ch 4.
- Daichi Ito's 16-year-old cousin (unnamed) photographed the "ghost bowl" with the back of Mei's head in frame; 8,000 likes. Seed for Ch 10 (the food-blog photo).
- Hiroshi's mother Nanami Shimizu (#0661) shells pickles at the counter. Rin Hayashi (#0821) is a former lifeguard and regular. Nanami Yamamoto (#0202) is Mei's friend, works the Tempozan ferry.

## Ch 5 (St Petersburg, S04)
- Nevsky Chamber Theatre (invented), 120th anniversary gala on the night of the first snow; chairman Dr Oleg Morozov (#0671, dentist, toothache); costume master Sergei Orlov (#0857) reads everyone's post.
- Dmitri's waltz: 24 bars, 3/4, with a one-eighth-note rest on the third beat of bar 7 that mirrors Anya's limp; he plays it nightly after the last curtain; programme billing "9. Waltz. Anonymous."
- Anya's left knee gave way six years ago; she teaches on the fourth floor above the theatre; pupil Yuri Orlov (#0594, 12) has an unmentioned cough (seed).
- Anya's sister Natalia Lebedev (#0409) keeps an inn on Kronstadt; best friend Oleg Popov (#0097, policeman).
- Anya's note to Dmitri: "Seventh bar. Third beat. I limp there. How long have you been listening? Six years is a very long time to hold a door. - A.P." Left under the stage-door lamp. Dmitri decides to go upstairs (four flights). Ch 17 has him ask her to dance.
- Anya's Register entry is #0017; Dmitri's is #0234.

## Ch 6 (Mexico City, S05; FE)
- Mural "La Lectora" (also called "La Señorita Que No Se Cansa"): 103 walls, eyes always closed; newest wall on Calle Zacatecas, Roma, with caption "TO THE ONE WHO READS: I HAVE NOT FINISHED YOUR EYES."
- Rafael first saw Camila in the Parque México in the rain two Novembers ago. Camila lives in Narvarte, has seen 91 walls and keeps a list. Her eyes are hazel (brown with a ring of green); she painted the first eye arc herself.
- Commandante Alejandro Hernández (#0125, vain policeman) believes the jaw is his; says he is saving to disappear to Oaxaca to keep goats; suspended Article 41 at the end.
- Marisol García (#0049), Lupita Hernández (#0738, Xochimilco chef).
- FE: Ermis sent her first message at 23:41 Yogyakarta / 22:41 Dhaka, one week after the Register opened ("Hello, Dhaka. You are one hour behind. I am not sorry about it."). Fayaz replied ("Sorry. (Reflex. It was not about the hour.) Hello, Yogyakarta. I would tell you everything, if I were free to. May I tell you a little?"). Ermis: "A little is how every pattern starts. Begin." Three lines total. Fayaz was at Bunny's tea stall, on a 14-hour work day.

## Ch 7 (Cairo, S06)
- Haddad & Sons, Khan el-Khalili (sign: "EST. WHEN GOD WAS YOUNG"; Omar has no sons).
- The first forgery: an 11 cm bronze "Ptolemaic Bastet" cat, sold to Layla three years ago for 3,000 Egyptian pounds; aged with vinegar, tea, cat-litter; left ear from a 1991 Brooklyn reproduction. It sits on the shelf above Layla's lamp.
- Layla's habit/signature: visible gold-thread mends on 41 objects in the Museum of Islamic Art.
- New piece: a modern brass pen-box (qalamdan) with silver inlay and a fake Mamluk mend copying Layla's gold seam including her wrist-catch error; tiny thuluth confession under the hinge. Layla's certificate: "Authentic - as to the hand that made it." She keeps it (not for sale); Omar will take her to dinner and she will require "provenance" for every word.
- Layla's nickname: "the Ice Pharaoh". Dina Shaker (#0540, 14) is Official Sweeper and Unofficial Witness; Tarek Hassan (#0407, 41, anxious librarian of Alexandria) remembers the exact day of every disaster; Karim Fouad (#0220) is Omar's potter friend; Sherif Sabry (#0325, 72) is the vain old novelist at Fishawi's.
